Route Overview

About This Route

Before it was a road, it was a footpath. The Choctaw and Chickasaw walked it for centuries. Then came the Kaintuck boatmen — traders who floated their flatboats down the Mississippi to New Orleans and walked the 500 miles home. The same worn corridor through the Mississippi and Tennessee wilderness, used by armies, explorers, and outlaws, is now a National Scenic Byway managed by the National Park Service.

"By Day 2 you stop waiting for the roar that never comes. The silence is the whole point."

For cyclists it's close to perfect. The grades are gentle, the pavement smooth, and the towns along the way still run on Southern time. Ride the lower 100 miles as your first self-guided tour, or commit to the full 444 and finish at the double-arch bridge outside Nashville.

Must-See

Six Stops That Define the Trace

Milepost 10.3 — Lower
Emerald Mound

Second largest Native American ceremonial mound in the US. Built around 1400 CE. Sunrise here is worth an early start.

Just off Milepost 41 — Lower
Windsor Ruins

23 haunting Corinthian columns in a field. All that remains of Mississippi's grandest antebellum mansion.

Milepost 122 — Middle
Cypress Swamp

A boardwalk loop through a water tupelo and bald cypress forest. The Trace at its most otherworldly.

Milepost 193.1 — Middle
French Camp

A historic inn that's been hosting Trace travelers since 1812. Still operating, still taking cyclists.

Milepost 385.9 — Upper
Meriwether Lewis Site

The gravesite and death site of the famous explorer. One of the most moving stops on the entire Trace.

Milepost 438 — Upper
Double-Arch Bridge

The northern terminus. Two soaring concrete arches over Highway 96. The finish line every full-Trace rider earns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need all three segments or can I just ride one?
Most people start with one segment. The Lower Trace is the best first tour — 3-4 days, the most dramatic history, and the flattest terrain. Buy the Lower guide, ride it, and come back for the rest when you're ready.
How are the three segments different?
The Lower has the most history and flattest terrain. The Middle is the quietest stretch with fewest services — deep forest, long days. The Upper has the most climbing and ends at the iconic double-arch bridge outside Nashville. All three share the same smooth NPS pavement with no commercial traffic.
When are the Middle and Upper guides available?
The Lower Trace guide is available now. Middle and Upper guides are in development. Click "Coming Soon" on either card to get notified when they're ready.
What's in each guide?
Day-by-day route description, PDF formatted for phone or print, GPX file for your device, turn-by-turn cue sheet, lodging recommendations, resupply points, and key waypoints. Everything a self-supported rider needs without a support vehicle.
Is the Natchez Trace good for beginners?
The Lower Trace is one of the best beginner cycling routes in the country. No commercial traffic, smooth NPS pavement, gentle grades. The biggest challenge isn't the hills — it's saddle conditioning. Start training your sit bones at least 4 weeks before you go.
