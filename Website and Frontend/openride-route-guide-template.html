Sample Itinerary — Lower Trace (3 Days)

Day 1

Arrival & Natchez City Exploration

0 miles · Natchez, MS

Arrive in Natchez — one of the oldest cities on the Mississippi River and the southern terminus of the Trace. Spend the afternoon exploring the historic downtown, the bluffs overlooking the Mississippi, and the antebellum mansions that earned this city its reputation as one of the wealthiest in pre-Civil War America. Dinner on the bluffs. Natchez has a surprisingly vibrant food scene — don't miss the Mississippi catfish.

Antebellum mansions Mississippi bluffs Southern cuisine B&B recommended

Day 2

Natchez to Port Gibson

~42 miles · ~2,100 ft elevation

Begin at the Natchez Visitor Center where the Parkway officially starts. The first miles wind through Spanish moss–draped forest before arriving at Emerald Mound — the second largest Native American ceremonial mound in the United States, built around 1400 CE. Standing atop it at sunrise is an experience you won't forget. Continue north to Port Gibson — a town Ulysses S. Grant reportedly declared "too beautiful to burn."

Emerald Mound Civil War history Port Gibson churches Old Trace paths

Day 3

Port Gibson to Rocky Springs

~40 miles · ~2,400 ft elevation

Today's highlight: a short detour to the Windsor Ruins — the hauntingly beautiful columns of what was once Mississippi's largest antebellum plantation house, destroyed by fire in 1890. Twenty-three Corinthian columns still stand in the forest, wrapped in vines. End your day at Rocky Springs — a ghost town free NPS campsite that is atmospheric and well maintained.

Windsor Ruins Rocky Springs ghost town Free NPS camping Photography

Where to Sleep

Free NPS Camping
Designated campsites along the Parkway. First come, first served. No fee. Bear boxes at some sites.
Bed & Breakfasts
Natchez and surrounding towns have exceptional B&Bs, often in historic antebellum homes. Book ahead in spring.
Small Town Motels
Budget-friendly options in Port Gibson, Kosciusko, Tupelo, and other trail towns. Cash sometimes preferred.
Mix & Match
Our recommended approach: camp 2–3 nights, splurge on a B&B in Natchez. Best of both worlds.

Essential Information

Getting There

  • Fly into Jackson, MS (JAN) or Nashville, TN (BNA)
  • Amtrak serves Natchez via the City of New Orleans
  • Shuttle services available for one-way trips
  • Most cyclists rent a car for shuttling

Food & Resupply

  • Plan carefully — resupply points are limited on the Trace
  • Stock up in Natchez, Port Gibson, Kosciusko, Tupelo
  • Carry 2–3 days of food between towns
  • Bring cash — many small towns are cash only

Road Conditions

  • No commercial trucks or vehicles — ever
  • Smooth NPS-maintained pavement throughout
  • Speed limit 50 mph — cars are generally courteous
  • Paved shoulders vary in width by section

What to Pack

  • Road bike or gravel bike — any gearing works
  • Panniers or bikepacking bags both fine
  • Insect repellent — essential in spring
  • Rain gear — afternoon storms common in spring
