About This Route

The Natchez Trace Parkway is one of America's most iconic and beginner-friendly long-distance cycling routes. Stretching 444 miles from Natchez, Mississippi to Nashville, Tennessee, this federally protected two-lane road carries no commercial traffic — no trucks, no semis, no billboards. Just smooth pavement, ancient forests, and 10,000 years of American history unfolding mile by mile.

Originally a series of trails used by the Choctaw and Chickasaw peoples, later walked by Kaintuck flatboatmen returning home from New Orleans, the Trace is now a National Scenic Byway managed by the National Park Service. For cyclists, it's close to perfect: gentle rolling terrain, designated free campsites, and a pace that invites deep exploration of the Deep South's food, culture, and past.

We offer multiple segment options — from a focused 3-day lower Trace experience around Natchez to a full end-to-end journey. Whether you have a long weekend or two weeks, this route delivers.

Sample Itinerary — Lower Trace (3 Days)

This is our most popular introduction to the Natchez Trace — rich in history, manageable distances, and ending in the charming river city of Natchez itself.

Day 1 — Arrival & Natchez City Exploration 0 miles riding
Natchez, MS

Arrive in Natchez — one of the oldest cities on the Mississippi River and the southern terminus of the Trace. Spend the afternoon exploring the historic downtown, the bluffs overlooking the mighty Mississippi, and the antebellum mansions that earned this city its reputation as one of the wealthiest in pre-Civil War America.

Dinner tonight should be at one of the restaurants along the bluffs. Natchez has a surprisingly vibrant food scene — don't miss the Mississippi catfish.

🏛️ Antebellum mansions 🌊 Mississippi bluffs 🍽️ Southern cuisine 🛏️ B&B recommended
Day 2 — Natchez to Port Gibson ~42 miles
~2,100 ft elevation

Begin your ride at the Natchez Visitor Center where the Parkway officially begins. The first miles wind through Spanish moss–draped forest before arriving at Emerald Mound — the second largest Native American ceremonial mound in the United States, built by the ancestors of the Natchez people around 1400 CE. Standing atop it at sunrise is an experience you won't forget.

Continue north to Port Gibson — a town Ulysses S. Grant reportedly declared "too beautiful to burn" during his 1863 campaign. Wander the main street, visit the famous First Presbyterian Church with its golden hand pointing to heaven, and settle in for the night at a local motel or B&B.

🏔️ Emerald Mound ⚔️ Civil War history ⛪ Port Gibson churches 🌿 Old Trace paths
Day 3 — Port Gibson to Rocky Springs ~40 miles
~2,400 ft elevation

Today's highlight is a short detour to the Windsor Ruins — the hauntingly beautiful columns of what was once Mississippi's largest antebellum plantation house, destroyed by fire in 1890. Twenty-three Corinthian columns still stand in the forest, wrapped in vines. It's one of the most otherworldly sights in the American South.

End your day at Rocky Springs — a ghost town that once held 2,600 residents before being abandoned after the Civil War and successive yellow fever epidemics. The free NPS campsite here is atmospheric and well maintained. Sit by a fire and listen to the forest reclaim what was once a thriving community.

🏛️ Windsor Ruins 👻 Rocky Springs ghost town ⛺ Free NPS camping 📸 Photography

Must-See Highlights

Emerald Mound

Second largest Native American ceremonial mound in the US. Built around 1400 CE. Milepost 10.3.

Windsor Ruins

23 haunting Corinthian columns — all that remains of Mississippi's grandest antebellum mansion. Just off Milepost 41.

Port Gibson

A town too beautiful to burn. Stunning antebellum architecture and the famous golden hand church steeple.

Rocky Springs

A ghost town reclaimed by nature. Free NPS campsite, original church still standing. Milepost 54.8.

Tupelo National Battlefield

Site of the July 1864 battle — one of the war's final major engagements in Mississippi. Milepost 259.7.

Meriwether Lewis Site

The gravesite and death site of the famous explorer — one of the Trace's most moving and mysterious stops. Milepost 385.9.

Natchez City

Begin or end here — antebellum mansions, Mississippi River bluffs, exceptional Southern food and hospitality.

Jackson, MS Area

The state capital offers excellent resupply, vibrant food scene, and Civil Rights history just off the Trace.

Where to Sleep

⛺

Free NPS Camping

Designated campsites along the Parkway. First come, first served. No fee. Bear boxes at some sites.

🏡

Bed & Breakfasts

Natchez and surrounding towns have exceptional B&Bs, often in historic antebellum homes. Book ahead in spring.

🏨

Small Town Motels

Budget-friendly options in Port Gibson, Kosciusko, Tupelo, and other trail towns. Cash sometimes preferred.

🔀

Mix & Match

Our recommended approach: camp 2–3 nights, splurge on a B&B in Natchez. Best of both worlds.

Essential Practical Information

Getting There

  • Fly into Jackson, MS (JAN) or Nashville, TN (BNA)
  • Amtrak serves Natchez via the City of New Orleans
  • Shuttle services available for one-way trips
  • Most cyclists rent a car for shuttling

Food & Resupply

  • Plan carefully — resupply points are limited on the Trace
  • Stock up in Natchez, Port Gibson, Kosciusko, Tupelo
  • Carry 2–3 days of food between towns
  • Bring cash — many small towns are cash only

Road Conditions

  • No commercial trucks or vehicles allowed — ever
  • Smooth NPS-maintained pavement throughout
  • Speed limit 50 mph — cars are generally courteous
  • Paved shoulders vary in width by section

Connectivity

  • Cell service is spotty in many sections
  • Download offline maps before you go
  • NPS visitor centers have WiFi
  • Emergency call boxes at some mileposts

What to Pack

  • Road bike or gravel bike — any gearing works
  • Panniers or bikepacking bags both fine
  • Insect repellent — essential in spring
  • Rain gear — afternoon storms common in spring

Safety

  • Ride with a buddy if possible on remote sections
  • Share your itinerary with someone at home
  • NPS rangers patrol regularly
  • Wildlife: deer, armadillos, wild turkey — watch at dawn/dusk
