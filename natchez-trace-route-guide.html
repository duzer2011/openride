The Experience

Deep South Discovery

The Natchez Trace Parkway is one of America's most iconic and beginner-friendly long-distance cycling routes. Stretching 444 miles from Natchez, Mississippi to Nashville, Tennessee, this federally protected two-lane road carries no commercial traffic — no trucks, no semis, no billboards. Just smooth pavement, ancient forests, and 10,000 years of American history unfolding mile by mile.

Originally a series of trails used by the Choctaw and Chickasaw peoples, later walked by Kaintuck flatboatmen returning home from New Orleans, the Trace is now a National Scenic Byway managed by the National Park Service. For cyclists, it's close to perfect: gentle rolling terrain, designated free campsites, and a pace that invites deep exploration of the Deep South's food, culture, and past.

The Journey

The 10-Day Slow Burn

Our recommended pace for those who want to see it all.

01 Day

Arrival in Natchez

0 miles · Orientation

Start your journey in the oldest settlement on the Mississippi River. Natchez is famous for its stunning antebellum architecture and legendary Southern hospitality. Check into a historic B&B and enjoy a sunset over the river from the bluff.

Natchez Bluff Stanton Hall Southern Dining
02 Day

Natchez to Port Gibson

~40 miles · ~1,800 ft elevation

Leave the city behind as you enter the Parkway. Today's highlights include Emerald Mound, the second-largest Native American ceremonial mound in the US, and Mt. Locust, a perfectly preserved 18th-century "stand" (inn).

Emerald Mound Mt. Locust Stand Loess Bluffs
03 Day

Port Gibson to Rocky Springs

~40 miles · ~2,400 ft elevation

Today's highlight: a short detour to the Windsor Ruins — the hauntingly beautiful columns of what was once Mississippi's largest antebellum plantation house. End your day at Rocky Springs — a ghost town free NPS campsite that is atmospheric and well maintained.

Windsor Ruins Ghost Town Free Camping
The Best Bits

Curated Highlights

Must-stop locations along the 444-mile corridor.

Milepost 10.3

Emerald Mound

Second largest Native American ceremonial mound in the US. Built around 1400 CE by the ancestors of the Natchez Indians.

Off Milepost 41

Windsor Ruins

23 haunting Corinthian columns — all that remains of Mississippi's grandest antebellum mansion, destroyed by fire in 1890.

Milepost 39.2

Port Gibson

"A town too beautiful to burn." Stunning architecutre and the famous First Presbyterian Church with its golden hand steeple.

Milepost 54.8

Rocky Springs

A ghost town reclaimed by nature. Free NPS campsite, historical markers, and an original 1837 church still standing.

Milepost 259.7

Tupelo Battlefield

Site of the July 1864 battle — one of the Civil War's final major engagements in Mississippi.

Milepost 385.9

Meriwether Lewis

The gravesite and death site of the famous explorer — one of the Trace's most moving and mysterious stops.

Preparation

Essential Info

What you need to know before you turn the pedals.

Getting There

  • Fly into Jackson (JAN) or Nashville (BNA)
  • Amtrak service available to Jackson, MS
  • One-way rental cars are standard for shuttling
  • Shuttle services available from local outfitters

Food & Resupply

  • Limited resupply directly on the Parkway
  • Detours required for most grocery stores
  • Carry 2 days of food for the southern sections
  • Filtered water available at most NPS stops

Road Conditions

  • Pavement is mostly glass-smooth
  • Strict 50mph speed limit — cars are slow
  • Cell service is spotty in the forests
  • No traffic signals or cross-traffic

What to Pack

  • 28mm+ tires recommended for comfort
  • Insect repellent (especially in May/June)
  • High-visibility lights for shaded sections
  • Lightweight rain shell for afternoon storms
